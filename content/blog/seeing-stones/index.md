I picked up [Seeing Stones](https://www.artofplay.com/products/seeing-stones) from Art of Play because I liked the premise: ambiguous symbols, no right answers, and a permission slip to use your imagination. Adam Rubin designed them. The set is five twelve-sided dice with sixty evocative little glyphs. Fortune telling for regular people.

This is a facilitation-friendly field guide — how to run a reading, not what any symbol *means*. That part belongs to the people in the room.

## What you need

- The [Seeing Stones set](https://www.artofplay.com/products/seeing-stones) (five dice, a cloth pouch, and a fold-out chart), **or** the virtual reader below
- A flat surface
- A question worth sitting with

## How to read

Adapted from Art of Play’s [companion guide](https://www.artofplay.com/pages/seeing-stones-companion):

1. Hold all five stones and shake them gently.
2. Think of a question — something unknown, non-trivial, and without malicious intent.
3. Spill the stones onto the table.
4. **Do not rearrange them.** Orientation and position matter.
5. Read the spread using zoom in / zoom out:
   - Notice the overall shape and vibe first.
   - Look at individual symbols (and neighbors).
   - Zoom out again — what connects them?
   - Revisit symbols in light of the whole, then draw a conclusion.

## Five ways to start

From the product packaging — pick a frame before you spill:

- Start with a **question** to see an answer
- Start with a **character** to make a story
- Start with a **problem** to find a solution
- Start with an **idea** to create a brainstorm
- Start with a **coincidence** to reveal a mystery

## What do you see?

The symbols are provocative and ambiguous. Their meanings shift depending on rotation and what sits beside them. Individually they mean nothing — together they could mean anything.

The chart that ships with the set says it plainly: *magic requires the active participation of imagination.* Resist the urge to rely on the chart.
